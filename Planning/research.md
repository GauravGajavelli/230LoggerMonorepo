  On n=20 and undergraduate research                                                                                      
                                                                                                                          
  20 opt-ins is tight but not disqualifying for undergraduate research — the bar is lower than a journal submission. A few
   honest considerations:                                                                                                 
                                                                                                                          
  - Personalized vs. generic: With ~20 total, splitting into two cells (personalized vs. generic control) gives you maybe 
  10 each, which is underpowered for statistical significance on exam scores. But you likely can't get exam score data    
  from the registrar easily anyway.                                                                                       
  - What you can show with n=20: Engagement metrics (did they open it, did they click drills, time on page) are within    
  reach even at small n. Qualitative patterns from the debugging journeys themselves are actually stronger evidence at    
  small n — a detailed case analysis of 5 students' debugging trajectories is publishable in an undergraduate research
  context.                                                                                                                
  - My recommendation: Don't split the groups. Give everyone personalized feedback if they submitted a tar, generic if  
  not. Use all 20 as a single treatment group. Your comparison becomes "here's what the system surfaced, here's the       
  engagement, here's the qualitative debugging pattern analysis" — which is honest and appropriate for the scope.
